# Opah 0.7.4

Open **Settings** for Connections and Notifications. **Check setup** checks
your saved connections and offers a next step when something needs attention.
Birdseye controls stay hidden when the video briefly buffers.

If you use a trusted private Frigate address that has no login, choose
**Connect without signing in**. This works with reverse proxy addresses on
custom ports. Thanks to [wingkinl](https://github.com/wingkinl) for the
[fork that demonstrated the change](https://github.com/VibeCodingAntagonist/opah-frigate-tv-app/commit/48203eff3ddd9ce2b5b9d136e338616f2b096107).

**Cameras & playback** puts startup and live video choices together. **Privacy**
groups your PIN and camera locks, and **About & updates** shows the installed
version and lets you check for an update.

Opah keeps the screen awake while you watch a camera, a saved camera view, or
a recording. When you leave playback, the TV can go idle again. **Monitor**
follows the **Allow sleep** or **Stay awake** choice you make in that session.
Your TV’s power settings can still put it in standby.

If you use Home Assistant buttons, open **Settings** > **Privacy** > **Protected areas** and choose
**Require PIN for Home Assistant buttons**. With it on, each button press asks
for your Opah PIN. With it off, only buttons that ask for a PIN in Home Assistant
are protected. If you have no Opah PIN, those buttons stay hidden. Choose
**Send request** to confirm any button.

You can make your first Home Assistant connection without an Opah PIN. Guest
Mode blocks setup and automation buttons. [Home Assistant setup guide](../home-assistant.md)
